Edition of 03/31/1999

World

The single currency is three months old. Since January the euro exists on bank statements and in the stock exchanges, but nobody has yet held one in their hand: coins and notes will arrive in 2002. In the meantime the shops print double prices, and calculators with the conversion key are selling out.

The balloon that wants to go round the world. Two pilots took off again from the mountains for the umpteenth attempt to circle the Earth without landing. Their capsule is the size of a lift. "We have food for three weeks and patience for two", one of them told the radio.

Floods in the south of the continent. The rivers burst their banks after ten days of rain: thousands of people have left their homes. International aid is arriving by helicopter, because the roads have gone with the water.

The space station grows. A second module has been joined to the first in orbit. Astronauts will live on board from next year. The cost, says the critics' estimate, is that of a medium-sized country's motorways.

Mobile phones: one in five. For the first time a European country has more mobile phones than fixed lines in homes. Sociologists are divided: freedom, or the end of the family phone in the hall where everybody could hear everything?

At home

Schools: the reform reaches the classrooms. From September the first two years of secondary school will have one more hour of computing a week. The teachers' unions ask for "computers first, then the hours": in many schools, they point out, the computer room is a cupboard with three machines and a printer that jams.

Hospitals: waiting lists down, but slowly. The ministry's figures show that waiting times for a specialist visit have fallen by eleven days in a year. Patients' associations are not impressed: "Eleven days out of ninety is not a revolution. It is a rounding".

Telephone: the new numbers. From next month, the area code becomes part of the number for local calls too. The phone company has sent a leaflet to every family; those who lost it can call the free information line, which, the company admits, "is very busy in the evening".

Lottery. No jackpot again this week. The prize, now the highest in years, has brought queues to tobacconists: one shopkeeper says he sold more tickets on Saturday than in the whole of February.

Sport

Cycling. The spring classics open on Saturday with the race along the coast. The favourite has a broken wrist and will start anyway: "I steer with the other hand", he joked at the presentation.

Basketball. The home team loses at the buzzer and drops to fourth place. The coach: "We played for forty minutes and switched off for four seconds". The fans at the exit were less philosophical.

Skiing. The season ends with little snow and a lot of rain. The resorts count the losses; the ski schools hope for Easter, "if it snows on the palms".
