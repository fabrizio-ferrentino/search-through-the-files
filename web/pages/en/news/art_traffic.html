Easter exodus: eleven million on the roads
The motorway police expect queues from Thursday afternoon. Their advice: leave early, stop often.
03/31/1999 · National desk

Eleven million people will leave home for the Easter weekend, according to the estimate of the motorway companies. The worst moments, as every year, will be Thursday afternoon and Monday evening, when everyone comes back at the same time.

The motorway police repeat the usual advice, which every year somebody forgets: check the tyres and the oil before leaving, do not overload the car, stop every two hours, and do not drive after a sleepless night. "Most of the accidents on holiday weekends are not caused by speed but by tiredness", says the head of the traffic police.

For those who want to know the traffic in real time there is the radio, with bulletins every half hour, and now the Internet: the motorway company's site publishes a map with the queues, updated every fifteen minutes. "Consult it before you leave, not while you drive", the company specifies.

Trains are almost full. The railways have added forty extra services; seats on the overnight trains to the south were sold out ten days ago.
