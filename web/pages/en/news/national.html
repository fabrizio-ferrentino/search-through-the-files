Schools: the reform reaches the classrooms. From September the first two years of secondary school will have one more hour of computing a week. The teachers' unions ask for "computers first, then the hours": in many schools, they point out, the computer room is a cupboard with three machines and a printer that jams.

Hospitals: waiting lists down, but slowly. The ministry's figures show that waiting times for a specialist visit have fallen by eleven days in a year. Patients' associations are not impressed: "Eleven days out of ninety is not a revolution. It is a rounding".

Telephone: the new numbers. From next month, the area code becomes part of the number for local calls too. The phone company has sent a leaflet to every family; those who lost it can call the free information line, which, the company admits, "is very busy in the evening".

Lottery. No jackpot again this week. The prize, now the highest in years, has brought queues to tobacconists: one shopkeeper says he sold more tickets on Saturday than in the whole of February.
