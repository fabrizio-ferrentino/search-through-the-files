Millennium bug: nine months to go, and the offices are still counting
One company in three has not checked its computers yet. The experts: the danger is the forgotten machine.
03/31/1999 · Technology desk

At midnight on the thirty-first of December, many computers will think it is 1900. The reason is a saving made decades ago, when memory was expensive: years were written with two digits only, and "00" is, for a machine, as likely to be the beginning of the century as its end.

The banks say they are ready. So do the airlines, the hospitals and the electricity companies, which have spent fortunes on checks. The problem, according to the survey published yesterday, is elsewhere: in the small offices, in the shops, in the homes. "The old PC in the corner that does the accounts, that nobody switches off and nobody remembers buying", says one of the consultants interviewed.

What should a family do? Not much, say the experts: switch the computer on after New Year's Day and check the date. If it says 1900, put it right by hand. Programs that calculate ages or due dates may get confused, so check them too. And keep a paper copy of anything important.

Some people are stocking up on candles and tinned food. The consumer associations advise calm: "The most likely damage is a wrong date on a receipt". A shopkeeper in the centre has put up a sign: "Millennium bug? We have the antidote: cash".
