Easter exodus: eleven million on the roads
The motorway police expect queues from Thursday afternoon. Their advice: leave early, stop often.
03/31/1999 · National desk

Eleven million people will leave home for the Easter weekend, according to the estimate of the motorway companies. The worst moments, as every year, will be Thursday afternoon and Monday evening, when everyone comes back at the same time.

The motorway police repeat the usual advice, which every year somebody forgets: check the tyres and the oil before leaving, do not overload the car, stop every two hours, and do not drive after a sleepless night. "Most of the accidents on holiday weekends are not caused by speed but by tiredness", says the head of the traffic police.

For those who want to know the traffic in real time there is the radio, with bulletins every half hour, and now the Internet: the motorway company's site publishes a map with the queues, updated every fifteen minutes. "Consult it before you leave, not while you drive", the company specifies.

Trains are almost full. The railways have added forty extra services; seats on the overnight trains to the south were sold out ten days ago.

Late frost, the orchards hold their breath
Two nights below freezing in the plains. Farmers lit fires between the rows to save the blossom.
03/30/1999 · National desk

Minus three degrees at five in the morning, with the peach trees in full blossom: for the farmers of the plains it is the worst possible combination. For two nights, from the main road, you could see small fires burning in the orchards, lit to keep the air a few degrees warmer around the flowers.

"We stayed up all night to keep them going", says a grower. "My father did it, my grandfather did it. You sit in the dark among the trees and you listen for the wind". The damage will only be known in a week, when the flowers that froze turn brown.

The farmers' association asks the region to declare a state of natural disaster in the worst-hit areas. The forecasters, meanwhile, promise a return to milder temperatures from the weekend.

The Cup goes to the wire
A goal in the ninetieth minute keeps the final round open. Next Sunday, all against all.
03/29/1999 · Sport desk

Ninety minutes of rain, a goalkeeper in a state of grace and, in the last minute, a header from the smallest man on the pitch: the Rovers beat City one-nil and the Cup, which seemed decided, goes to the last round.

"I closed my eyes and jumped", the scorer told the reporters, still covered in mud. His coach was less romantic: "We practised that corner for three weeks. Three weeks. Ask the boys".

Next Sunday four teams will play for two places in the final. The tickets for the home match were sold out in an afternoon; the club has promised a big screen in the car park for those who stayed outside.
