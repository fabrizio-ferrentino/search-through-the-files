The Internet café opens in the old cinema
Eight computers, coffee and a modem that never sleeps: the young people of the town have a new meeting place.
03/29/1999 · National desk

Where the cinema's screen used to be there is now a long table with eight computers, and where the projector was there is a coffee machine. The town's first Internet café opened on Saturday, and by the evening there was a queue at the door.

An hour costs the same as two coffees. "Most of them come to chat", says the owner, who was a projectionist in the same room for twenty years. "They write to people they have never seen, in other cities, in other countries. Then they go out into the square and do not say hello to each other". He laughs, but not too much.

Not only the young. On Sunday morning a group of pensioners came to look up the train timetables, and a lady asked for help to write her first e-mail to a grandson who lives abroad. "She dictated it to me. Three pages. I told her that e-mails are usually shorter. She said: then he will read it twice".

The café is open every day from ten in the morning to midnight. On Fridays, the owner says, he would like to show an old film on the wall again, "for those who come to look at a screen anyway".
