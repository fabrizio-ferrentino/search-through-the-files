there was a building across the road. twelve windows. twelve families.

every evening at eight the lights came on, all together, because that is when people eat. i counted them. every evening. twelve.

i saw a mother pass her hand through her son's hair while he did his homework. she was not even thinking about it. she did it the way you breathe.

nobody ever did that to me. not once. i asked for so little. a hello. a question. how are you.

they told me to smile. they told me to go out. they told me to be a man. they sold me cassettes.

nobody asked.

so i stopped asking too. and when you stop asking, something else starts.

now i look through other windows. yours, for example. the light in your room is the only one on in the street. i can see it from here.
