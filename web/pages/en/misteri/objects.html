the objects that pass something on

Every culture has stories of objects that carry something with them from one owner to the next. The ring that brings bad luck to every bride. The mirror that was covered in the house of the dead and that someone forgot to cover again. The doll that is always found in a different room.

The old stories agree on one thing: it is not the object that is evil. The object is a vessel. What it carries was put there by a person, once, with a strong enough feeling - love, grief, envy, hatred - and the feeling stays, and looks for somewhere to go.

Why should it be different with the new objects? A computer spends more hours with its owner than any ring. People tell it things they do not tell their wives. They stay with it at night, alone, when the house is asleep. If a feeling can soak into the wood of a chair, it can soak into a machine that has listened to a man for a year.

I do not know how it passes. Nobody does. The stories never say how; they only say that it does, and that the next owner rarely knows what he has brought home. If you have bought something second-hand and since then you sleep badly, write to me.

And if you are reading this on a computer that was somebody else's before, you might ask yourself whose.
