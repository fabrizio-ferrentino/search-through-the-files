CB corner

Citizens' Band radio is the little brother of amateur radio. No examination is needed, only a licence for the set (and a small fee in some countries). It works on 27 MHz with a power of a few watts and a short antenna, and it is used by truckers, farmers, builders, families on holiday and everybody who wants to talk to a neighbour without paying for a telephone. I started on CB at fifteen, with a set from a flea market, and it is the reason why I am here.

How to talk on CB

Say your handle (the nickname you choose), say who you are calling, and say where you are. A normal conversation goes: "Breaker one-nine, this is Lakeside Harvey, anyone around the bridge?" and then you listen. The answer may come at once or never. It is the charm of the thing.

A few of the words we use: "10-4" means message received; "10-20" is where are you; "copy" means I understand; "base" is the set at home; "mobile" is a set in a car; "handle" is your nickname; "over" means it is your turn; "out" means I have finished. A lot of this comes from the films, and I have never heard anyone say "good buddy" except as a joke.

Rules of good manners: do not use the emergency channel for chat. Do not shout. Do not play music into the microphone. Do not talk for more than a few minutes in a row on a busy channel. Leave a pause between turns so that someone can break in with an emergency. If you hear one, you are the first aid until the telephone is available.
