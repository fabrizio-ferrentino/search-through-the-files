Build a 20 metre dipole, step by step

The dipole is the most useful antenna that you can build at home: two pieces of wire, a centre insulator, two end insulators and a coaxial cable. Mine has worked every evening for four years, through two storms and one very angry magpie. The notes below are for the 20 metre band, around 14.2 MHz. For other bands, change the length with the formula.

The formula

Total length in feet = 468 divided by the frequency in MHz. For 14.2 MHz that is 468 / 14.2 = 33 feet. Each side is half of that: 16 feet 6 inches. Cut the wire a little longer, about 6 inches more on each side, and tune by shortening.

What you need
  • About 40 feet of stranded copper wire, insulated or bare
  • Three insulators (porcelain, or even plastic from a clothes line)
  • 30 to 60 feet of coaxial cable of 50 ohms
  • Rope for the ends, tied to a tree, a mast or a chimney, 25 to 40 feet high
  • A standing wave ratio meter, borrowed from the club
  • A plug for the radio end, and a roll of self-amalgamating tape
Steps
  1. Cut two pieces of wire, each 17 feet. This is a bit long on purpose.
  2. Connect the centre of the coaxial cable to one wire and the screen to the other, at the middle insulator. Wrap the joints well and cover with tape: rain gets in everywhere.
  3. Tie the end of each wire to an end insulator and fix a rope to each.
  4. Raise the antenna as high as you can. Higher is better. The dipole should be as straight as possible, but a small slope does not matter.
  5. Measure the SWR at 14.1, 14.2 and 14.3 MHz. If it is lowest at the bottom of the band, the antenna is too long; if lowest at the top, too short.
  6. Cut about one inch at a time from each end, and measure again. A good antenna has a ratio below 1.5.

Notes. A dipole made like this also works on the 40 metre band if you accept a high ratio and a tuner, and on the 15 metre band on its third multiple. A friend uses the same wire for 80 metres, but it is a very long piece and he has to string it across the garden and into the neighbour's apple tree, with permission.

Safety. Never put up an antenna where it may fall on a power line. Never. Not even once. If the antenna can fall on a line, make it shorter or put it somewhere else. Disconnect the coaxial cable from the radio during thunderstorms, and wear gloves when you climb the ladder. My wife holds the ladder and makes a comment about every step.

The vertical. My 10 metre vertical is made of an aluminium tube 8 feet 4 inches long on a wooden post, with four radials. It works better than it looks. It looks like a curtain rod.
