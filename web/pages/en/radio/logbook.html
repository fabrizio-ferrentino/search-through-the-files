The logbook

Every contact goes into the paper logbook the same evening, in pencil, and I copy it into the computer once a week. The computer can break; the pencil does not. Here are my last eight contacts. UTC is the world time that all radio amateurs use: for Millbrook, in winter, it is local time plus five hours; from April, plus four. RST is the report: readability (1 to 5), strength (1 to 9), tone (1 to 9, for Morse).

Awards. Worked All Continents: five of six, missing only the one with the penguins. The certificate for 50 countries came in November. It is on the wall, a bit crooked.

How to read the log. The date, the time (UTC), the frequency in megahertz, the mode (SSB is voice, CW is Morse), the call sign of the other station, the report I gave, the place, and my notes. If your call is in the log and you did not receive a card, tell me: it may have been lost in the post.

A good contact. On 03/27/1999 I made the contact with South Africa on 10 metres. The signal came out of the noise at 8:55 PM local time like a door opening. We spoke for twelve minutes. He said it was summer there. I said it was raining. Neither of us believed the other.

QSL cards

A QSL card is a postcard that two radio amateurs exchange to confirm a contact. I have 600 of them. My own card has a drawing of the Millbrook iron bridge in black on yellow, made by my niece when she was nine. A few from my wall:

  • DJ5HTA, Kassel: a fountain with a statue and a pigeon. The pigeon is the only coloured thing on the card.
  • EA4QLM, Madrid: a bull, a guitar and a fan. I got it after my first Spanish contact in 1996; the card took four months.
  • UA3TRD, Moscow: a black-and-white photograph of a radio tower. Hand-stamped, hand-signed. A masterpiece of the type.

The card of contact number 3,400 has not yet arrived; the space on the wall is ready. To get a card, send your own first, with a self-addressed envelope and something for the postage. The best card I ever received was a ballpoint drawing of a boat by a man on the coast. At the bottom: "Thank you for waiting for me to find the right words in English." We had spoken for forty minutes. I will keep that one.

The Millbrook Amateur Radio Society

The Society was founded in 1971 by seven men and a borrowed receiver. Today we have 43 members, from a boy of fourteen to a lady of eighty-one who still sends Morse faster than any of us. We meet on the first Thursday of every month at 8:00 PM, in the back room of the fire station, and the coffee is made by the youngest member, who is not allowed to forget the sugar.

In the flood of 1991 the Society kept the town hall and the hospital in touch for four days. Join us: $12 a year, $6 for students. There is no test to join, only a handshake. The secretary is Mrs. Wheeler, care of the fire station, Millbrook. The president is the one with the beard. There are two with beards; the president is the taller.
