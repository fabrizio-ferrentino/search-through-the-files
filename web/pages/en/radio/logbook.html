The logbook

Every contact goes into the paper logbook the same evening, in pencil, and I copy it into the computer once a week. The computer can break; the pencil does not. Here are my last eight contacts. UTC is the world time that all radio amateurs use: for Millbrook, in winter, it is local time plus five hours; from April, plus four. RST is the report: readability (1 to 5), strength (1 to 9), tone (1 to 9, for Morse).

Awards. Worked All Continents: five of six, missing only the one with the penguins. The certificate for 50 countries came in November. It is on the wall, a bit crooked.

How to read the log. The date, the time (UTC), the frequency in megahertz, the mode (SSB is voice, CW is Morse), the call sign of the other station, the report I gave, the place, and my notes. If your call is in the log and you did not receive a card, tell me: it may have been lost in the post.

A good contact. On 03/27/1999 I made the contact with South Africa on 10 metres. The signal came out of the noise at 8:55 PM local time like a door opening. We spoke for twelve minutes. He said it was summer there. I said it was raining. Neither of us believed the other.
