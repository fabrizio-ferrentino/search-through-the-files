Teaching

This semester (spring 1999) I teach two courses and supervise one seminar. Lectures are in Lecture Hall B of the Hobbs Building unless stated otherwise.

MATH 341 - Numerical Analysis I

Second year, 6 credits, Monday and Thursday 10:00 AM to 12:00 noon.

  • Floating-point arithmetic, rounding and cancellation
  • Roots of nonlinear equations: bisection, Newton, secant
  • Direct methods for linear systems: Gaussian elimination, pivoting, LU decomposition
  • Iterative methods: Jacobi, Gauss-Seidel, convergence
  • Interpolation and least squares
  • Numerical integration: trapezoid, Simpson, Gauss rules

Textbook: W. Brandt, Numerical Methods for Beginners, second edition, Lakeside Press, 1994. It is also available in the library, three copies, reference shelf. Students may also use any other introductory book; I recommend the one by Professor Holm, which is clearer but more expensive.

Assessment: a written exam (three hours, no calculators with memory) and a short programming project. The project may be written in Pascal, C or Fortran. Please do not hand in a program that only runs on your own machine.

MATH 452 - Numerical Methods for Differential Equations

Fourth year, 6 credits, Tuesday 2:00 PM to 4:00 PM and Friday 9:00 AM to 11:00 AM. Prerequisites: MATH 341 and Ordinary Differential Equations.

  • One-step methods, Runge-Kutta, error control
  • Multistep methods and stability regions
  • Stiff problems and implicit methods
  • Boundary value problems, shooting and finite differences

Assessment: an oral exam of about thirty minutes and a report on a problem of your choice.

Seminar - Computing in Practice

Open to third-year students. We meet on alternate Wednesdays at 4:00 PM in Room 214 to discuss papers and problems that students bring. Attendance is voluntary and the coffee is bad.

Rules for programming projects

The program must read its data from a file, not from the keyboard. It must print the number of iterations. It must not use a library routine for the thing it is supposed to compute: if the project asks for a linear solver, you write the solver. You must hand in the source code on a floppy disk, with your name on the label, plus a printout. Programs that do not compile are returned without comment.
