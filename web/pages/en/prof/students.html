Thinking of writing a thesis with me?

I accept three or four students a year. The thesis should include a computing part: you write programs, run them, and look at the results with a critical eye. A thesis that consists only of copied theory will not be accepted. Come to my office hours with a topic or, if you do not have one, with the list of the courses you liked.

Some advice, from experience:

  • Start early. The students who come in the last semester always say they need two more months.
  • Back up your work. Not once: twice. On two different floppy disks, in two different places. Every year somebody loses a chapter.
  • Write in short sentences. A thesis is not a novel, and I read it with a pencil.
  • Test your program on the case you know the answer to before you test it on the case you do not.
  • Do not use a library routine for the thing you are studying. You will learn nothing and it will be obvious.

Tutoring

Second-year students who find the course hard can come to the tutoring hour, held by my assistant Dr. Eklund in the computer room on Fridays from 2:00 to 4:00 PM. It is free and nobody will be told you came.

The computer room

The department computer room has twelve machines, a laser printer and a scanner that works on odd days. It is open from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM on weekdays. Please do not install games, even if the network is slow. Food and drink are forbidden near the keyboards, and I mean the coffee too.

Exam statistics, March 1999

In the winter session 128 students enrolled in MATH 341, 94 took the written exam and 61 passed. The average mark was 21 out of 30. The most frequent mistake was to forget the pivoting in question 2. The most original mistake was a student who solved the system correctly but copied the wrong matrix from the board; this was also the most upsetting mistake, for the student.
