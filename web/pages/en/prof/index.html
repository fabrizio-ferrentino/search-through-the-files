Welcome

I am a professor of numerical analysis in the Department of Mathematics of Lakeland University, in Millbrook. My office is in the Hobbs Building, second floor, between the photocopier and the broken coffee machine. I have been here since 1984. Before that I was a research assistant at the Institute of Applied Computation, and before that a student, like everybody else.

These pages contain what students usually ask me for: the syllabus of my courses, the exam dates, the notes of my lectures, the list of my papers and the times when I can be found in my office. They are not meant to be pretty. If you look for something flashy, this is not the place.

Research interests

  • Numerical solution of linear systems, in particular iterative methods for sparse matrices
  • Error analysis and stability of floating-point computation
  • Numerical methods for ordinary differential equations (stiff problems)
  • Teaching numerical methods with small computers: what a student can learn on a machine with 8 MB of memory

Teaching

This semester (spring 1999) I teach two courses and supervise one seminar. Lectures are in Lecture Hall B of the Hobbs Building unless stated otherwise.

MATH 341 - Numerical Analysis I

Second year, 6 credits, Monday and Thursday 10:00 AM to 12:00 noon.

  • Floating-point arithmetic, rounding and cancellation
  • Roots of nonlinear equations: bisection, Newton, secant
  • Direct methods for linear systems: Gaussian elimination, pivoting, LU decomposition
  • Iterative methods: Jacobi, Gauss-Seidel, convergence
  • Interpolation and least squares
  • Numerical integration: trapezoid, Simpson, Gauss rules

Textbook: W. Brandt, Numerical Methods for Beginners, second edition, Lakeside Press, 1994. It is also available in the library, three copies, reference shelf. Students may also use any other introductory book; I recommend the one by Professor Holm, which is clearer but more expensive.

Assessment: a written exam (three hours, no calculators with memory) and a short programming project. The project may be written in Pascal, C or Fortran. Please do not hand in a program that only runs on your own machine.

MATH 452 - Numerical Methods for Differential Equations

Fourth year, 6 credits, Tuesday 2:00 PM to 4:00 PM and Friday 9:00 AM to 11:00 AM. Prerequisites: MATH 341 and Ordinary Differential Equations.

  • One-step methods, Runge-Kutta, error control
  • Multistep methods and stability regions
  • Stiff problems and implicit methods
  • Boundary value problems, shooting and finite differences

Assessment: an oral exam of about thirty minutes and a report on a problem of your choice.

Questions I am asked often

Can I do the oral exam before the written one?
No.
Can I use a programmable calculator?
You can use it for a project. You cannot use it at the exam.
May I hand in the project late?
Yes, until the day of the oral exam. After that it counts for the next session.
Is the course going to change next year?
A little. I want to add a lecture on the double precision format, now that every machine has it.
Do you have the old exams?
The last five years are in the library, on the reference shelf, in a grey folder. Answers are not included, on purpose.
