41,208 members and growing. Whoever you are and whatever you want to show the world - your dog, your stamps, your band, your recipe for pound cake, the history of your street - HomePlot gives you a plot of your own on the Web, free, with 5 MB of space, an address of your own and a counter. No fee, no contract, no cleverness required.

HomePlot began in the spring of 1996 as a single computer in a spare room in Millbrook, with a free software server and a lot of hope. Now we have eleven servers, six neighbourhoods and a small team that answers your letters on weekdays. We are not part of any large company; we are paid by the banners you see at the top of the page, and by those of our members who choose the Plus plan. Please do not mind the banners: they pay for the electricity.

How it works in three steps
  1. Choose a neighbourhood. Pick the one that fits your page: Rocket Park for computers and science fiction, Paw Street for pets, Melody Lane for music, Garden Row for gardens and cooking, Wheels Avenue for cars and bikes, Study Hall for school and hobbies. See the neighbourhoods page.
  2. Sign up. Write to us a letter or a postcard with your full name, postal address, year of birth, the neighbourhood you want and the name you want for your page (lower case, no spaces; the address will be members.homeplot.com/~yourname). The online sign-up form is suspended during the move to the new server. You get a welcome letter by post within ten days, with your password and your first page already in place.
  3. Build your page. Write it with any editor, or with Notepad, and send it to us by FTP. Read the tips if you have never done it. There is nothing you cannot learn on a rainy afternoon.

The six neighbourhoods

A neighbourhood is a group of pages about the same sort of thing. It helps visitors to find you, and it helps us to move you if one server gets full. You choose it when you sign up, and you can change it once a year by writing to us. Each neighbourhood has a mayor (a volunteer member) who reads the pages sent to its directory and tells us if somebody is breaking the rules. The mayors are not paid and are very patient.

Free plan or Plus?

The free plan gives you 5 MB, a hit counter, a guestbook and a banner in the corner of the page. Plus gives you 20 MB, no banner, a bigger traffic limit and a reply to your letters within two working days: $4.95 a month, paid by post with a cheque or a money order. We ask for a postal address because it is the only way we have of knowing that the member is a real person.

Ten tips from our mayors
  1. Start small. One good page is better than ten empty ones marked "under construction". Nobody likes the little man with the shovel, and everybody has seen him.
  2. Keep the pictures small. A visitor with a 28.8 modem needs about four seconds for each 10 KB. A background of 80 KB means twenty seconds of grey.
  3. Use readable colours. Yellow text on a white background is a hobby for people who dislike their visitors.
  4. Do not use more than two fonts. The page is not a ransom note.
  5. Say what the page is about in the first line, and put your name on it.
  6. Test it with both of the common browsers. They do not agree about tables.
  7. Do not use blinking text for more than a word. A whole page that blinks is a punishment.
  8. Put a link back to the main page on every page, and write the date of the last change at the bottom.
  9. Back up your files on a floppy disk. We back up the server every week, but we are not a miracle.

To send your files you use an FTP client: give it the name of our server, your page name as the user name and your password, then drag the files from your computer to your plot. If the connection stops halfway, send the file again. Each plot has a hit counter; it counts every load, including yours. A new page with ninety visits in a day is probably you pressing Reload.

The rules of the neighbourhood (last changed 03/01/1999)
  • Your page is yours, and so is the responsibility for it. The mayors read the pages in the directory.
  • No adult material, no violence for its own sake, no pages that offend people for what they are.
  • No selling. The free plan is for private pages; a page that tries to sell something is closed after one warning.
  • Copyright: do not put on your page text, pictures, songs or programs that belong to somebody else unless they said yes. "I found it on the Web" is not a yes.
  • Members under thirteen need the written permission of a parent. Pages by children must not give the home address or telephone number of the child.
  • The banner stays. Taking it off the page is the only offence for which we do not give a warning.
  • A plot that has not been changed or visited for twelve months is closed after a letter to the postal address.

We keep the postal address, the year of birth and the page name of each member. We do not sell them or lend them, and we do not know who would want them. If you ask us in writing, we erase everything about you within thirty days. HomePlot is run by a small team on borrowed machines: if the server breaks and your files are lost we will help you rebuild, but we cannot pay for the lost evenings.

Frequently asked questions

Is it really free? Yes. We make our money from the banners and from the Plus plan. You never receive a bill unless you ask for Plus.
Can I put music on my page? MIDI files, yes. MP3 files are too big for the free plan. If you put music, please let the visitor turn it off.
Can I have my own name, like www.mypage.com? Not on the free plan: registering a name costs money and we have not decided how to ask for it.
What is the "500 Internal Server Error" I get when I use a form? The script that handles the form did not answer. During the move to the new server some scripts are switched off. Nothing is lost on your side; try again in a few days.
I forgot my password. Write to us from the postal address you gave. We will send a new one by letter; it takes about ten days.
How many people work at HomePlot? Five, two of them part-time and one of them the founder's sister.
Do you have a telephone number? No, because with a telephone we would never write the pages. Letters only, to HomePlot, Mill Street 3, Millbrook.
What happens if HomePlot closes? We will tell you with at least two months' notice and help you to move your files. We do not intend to close; we have just bought a second photocopier.

Looking for a page? The member pages directory lists the pages of some of our members, with their neighbourhood and a short description. The directory is updated by hand once a month; the search service is suspended while we move to the new server.

Do you have a question? Read the FAQ first: 80 per cent of our letters ask something that is written there.
