Who I am

I was born in 1921 in a house with a wood stove and no running water. I learned to cook at seven, standing on a chair next to my mother. At twenty I married a man who could only make spaghetti with nothing, and for fifty-two years I cooked for him, for our four children, and then for our nine grandchildren.

He has been gone for six years now. The first months I did not cook at all: what is the point of cooking for one? Then one Sunday the grandchildren arrived without warning, all of them, hungry, and I understood that the point is the others. Since then, every Sunday the table is full.

Luke, my eldest grandson, is the one who studies computers. At Christmas he gave me this page and taught me to use the mouse, which I still call "the little animal". I do not write on the computer myself: I dictate, he writes. But I read every letter, with my glasses on.

If you are alone and you have read this far, here is a piece of advice from an old woman: cook something tonight, even something small, and lay the table properly. Then, one of these days, invite someone. People say yes more often than you think.
