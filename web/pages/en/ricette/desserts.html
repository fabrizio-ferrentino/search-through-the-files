Desserts

  • My mother's Easter cake

Breakfast cake. The one that is always in the tin. Three eggs, the weight of the eggs in sugar, in flour and in butter, a lemon's zest, a spoonful of baking powder. Beat, pour, forty minutes in the oven. It is called a "pound cake" because my mother weighed everything on the old scale with the weights. I still use it.

Rice pudding. For children who are sad. Rice cooked slowly in milk with sugar and a cinnamon stick until it is soft and creamy. Served warm in a cup. My grandchildren ask for it when they are sad, and sometimes when they are not, to see if I believe them. I always believe them.

Baked apples. Remove the core, put a little sugar, a piece of butter and a raisin or two inside. In the oven until the skin wrinkles. The whole house smells of autumn.

Fried pastry for Carnival. Flour, eggs, a drop of grappa, sugar. Roll it thin, cut it in strips, fry it, and cover it with icing sugar. You will have icing sugar everywhere until Easter.
