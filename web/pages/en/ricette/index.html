Granny Rose's Kitchen
recipes written as I make them, with my hands and my glasses on

Good morning, or good evening, depending on when you read me. My name is Rose, I am seventy-eight years old, and this is my kitchen on the computer. My grandson Luke made it for me at Christmas, because I was always copying the recipes by hand for everyone and my wrist hurt.

Here you will find the recipes of my family, the ones I learned from my mother and the ones I invented when there was nothing in the house. They are written the way I make them. When I say "a little", it means a little. When I say "until it is ready", it means you have to look at it.

Sunday lasagne

For eight people, or six hungry ones

  • fresh egg pasta sheets: four eggs and four hundred grams of flour, if you make it yourself
  • the meat sauce: minced beef and pork, a carrot, an onion, a celery stick, a glass of red wine, two jars of tomato passata
  • the white sauce: a litre of milk, eighty grams of butter, eighty of flour, nutmeg
  • grated cheese, a lot
  1. The sauce first. It wants time, so start at eight in the morning. Chop carrot, onion and celery very small, the smaller the better, and let them go soft in oil without colouring. Add the meat and break it up with a wooden spoon until it is all brown. Pour in the wine and let it evaporate: the kitchen will smell of wine and you will think you have made a mistake. You have not. Add the tomato, salt, and leave it on the lowest flame for at least two hours, stirring now and then. If it gets too thick, a little water.
  2. The white sauce. Melt the butter, add the flour and stir for a minute. Pour in the warm milk all at once and whisk, whisk, whisk. When it coats the spoon it is ready. Salt and a grating of nutmeg.
  3. Putting it together. A spoonful of sauce at the bottom of the dish, so it does not stick. Then pasta, meat sauce, white sauce, cheese. Pasta, meat sauce, white sauce, cheese. As many layers as you have patience. On the top, more white sauce and more cheese, so it makes a crust.
  4. In the oven at 180 degrees for forty minutes, until the edges are brown and it bubbles. Then - this is the hardest part - leave it to rest for a quarter of an hour before cutting it. If you cut it at once, it runs everywhere. My grandchildren never wait. Every Sunday it runs everywhere.

My mother's Easter cake

For a big round tin

My mother made it on Holy Thursday, and on Holy Thursday I make it, because it needs two days to rest before Easter. The whole house waited for it: the smell of orange blossom came out from under the cloth and reached the street.

First courses

Broth for when you are ill. A piece of beef, a piece of chicken, a carrot, a celery stick, an onion with a clove stuck in it, cold water, salt. On the smallest flame for three hours, without boiling: it must smile, not laugh. Skim it. Strain it. Cook small pasta in it, and eat it in bed with a blanket. It does not cure anything, but it helps everything.

Pasta and beans. The night before, put the dried beans in water. The next day, cook them slowly with a potato, a little tomato, a rosemary sprig. Mash half of them with a fork, so the soup becomes thick. Add short pasta and cook it inside. Pepper, a drop of oil on top. On the second day it is better.

Main courses

Chicken with potatoes, as on Sundays in the country. Chicken in pieces, potatoes in pieces, rosemary, sage, garlic in its skin, oil, salt. Everything in the same tin, in the oven, for an hour and a quarter. Turn everything over halfway. The potatoes under the chicken are the best part, and in my house there was always a fight for them.

Meatballs. Minced meat, a slice of stale bread soaked in milk and squeezed, an egg, cheese, parsley, a little garlic, salt. Mix with your hands - not with a spoon, with your hands, the meat must feel that someone is there. Make balls the size of a walnut. Fry them, or cook them in tomato sauce, or both: first fried, then in the sauce. That is how my mother did them, and that is how they disappear before they reach the table.

Desserts

Breakfast cake. The one that is always in the tin. Three eggs, the weight of the eggs in sugar, in flour and in butter, a lemon's zest, a spoonful of baking powder. Beat, pour, forty minutes in the oven. It is called a "pound cake" because my mother weighed everything on the old scale with the weights. I still use it.

Rice pudding. For children who are sad. Rice cooked slowly in milk with sugar and a cinnamon stick until it is soft and creamy. Served warm in a cup. My grandchildren ask for it when they are sad, and sometimes when they are not, to see if I believe them. I always believe them.

Your letters

Martha, from the city - 03/21/1999
Dear Granny Rose, I made your lasagne for my husband's birthday. It ran everywhere. He said it was the best of his life. Thank you.

Dear Martha, it always runs everywhere. That is how you know it was good. Rose
a student - 03/10/1999
Granny, I live alone for university and I only know how to make pasta with butter. Is there something easy?

My dear boy, try the midnight spaghetti, then the omelette. And phone your mother, she misses you more than you think. Rose

Who I am

I was born in 1921 in a house with a wood stove and no running water. I learned to cook at seven, standing on a chair next to my mother. At twenty I married a man who could only make spaghetti with nothing, and for fifty-two years I cooked for him, for our four children, and then for our nine grandchildren.

If you try a recipe, write to me and tell me how it went. Luke reads me the letters on Sunday when he comes to lunch, and then I answer, and he writes. You can read some of them in your letters.

A kitchen where someone cooks is never empty.
