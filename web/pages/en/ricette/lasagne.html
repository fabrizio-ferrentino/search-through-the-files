Sunday lasagne

For eight people, or six hungry ones
fresh egg pasta sheets: four eggs and four hundred grams of flour, if you make it yourself
the meat sauce: minced beef and pork, a carrot, an onion, a celery stick, a glass of red wine, two jars of tomato passata
the white sauce: a litre of milk, eighty grams of butter, eighty of flour, nutmeg
grated cheese, a lot

The sauce first. It wants time, so start at eight in the morning. Chop carrot, onion and celery very small, the smaller the better, and let them go soft in oil without colouring. Add the meat and break it up with a wooden spoon until it is all brown. Pour in the wine and let it evaporate: the kitchen will smell of wine and you will think you have made a mistake. You have not. Add the tomato, salt, and leave it on the lowest flame for at least two hours, stirring now and then. If it gets too thick, a little water.

The white sauce. Melt the butter, add the flour and stir for a minute. Pour in the warm milk all at once and whisk, whisk, whisk. When it coats the spoon it is ready. Salt and a grating of nutmeg.

Putting it together. A spoonful of sauce at the bottom of the dish, so it does not stick. Then pasta, meat sauce, white sauce, cheese. Pasta, meat sauce, white sauce, cheese. As many layers as you have patience. On the top, more white sauce and more cheese, so it makes a crust.

In the oven at 180 degrees for forty minutes, until the edges are brown and it bubbles. Then - this is the hardest part - leave it to rest for a quarter of an hour before cutting it. If you cut it at once, it runs everywhere. My grandchildren never wait. Every Sunday it runs everywhere.
