Letters from readers

Since January I have received thirty-one letters about this page, one of them in green ink. Here are some of them with my answers. If you want to write, use the form at the bottom: I read everything, and answer what I can.

Margaret, Millbrook, 03/27/1999: My son says it is all a hoax invented by computer firms to sell new computers. Is he right?
Roy: Partly. Some firms do sell new things with fear. But the problem is real; it is just smaller than they say, and more spread out than the newspapers say. Do the checklist and sleep well.
Giovanni, Lakeview, 03/22/1999: I have a very old computer, from 1986. Will it die at midnight?
Roy: A machine that old probably has only two digits for the year, so it will show a strange date. But it will not explode. Save your files, check the date at the new year, and set it by hand.
A teacher, 03/18/1999: Can you come and talk to our class? We are studying the subject.
Roy: With pleasure. I will bring my charts and the washing machine's letter. Call the school secretary to fix the day.
Peter, Riverside, 03/12/1999: I run a small plumbing shop. My invoices are in a program from 1992. What do I do?
Roy: Call the people who sold it. If they are gone, make a copy of all the data and ask a computer shop to look. Do this in the summer, not in December: in December the shops will be full of people like you.
Anna, 03/08/1999: I am afraid of the lift in my building, which is very old. Should I be?
Roy: Lifts have mechanical locks. If the electronics stop, they stop; they do not fall. Ask the administrator if the lift has been tested. If it does not satisfy you, take the stairs, which is healthy anyway.
Someone who signs T., 03/03/1999: You are a fool. The date problem was solved years ago. Stop worrying people.
Roy: I hope you are right, T. I will be very happy to have worried people for nothing. I would write a page saying that I was wrong, and I would celebrate with a cake.
