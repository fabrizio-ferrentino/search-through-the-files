The checklist: twenty things, in order

Print this page and put it on the refrigerator. Tick each box with a pencil. The things are in order of importance, as I see it, not in order of difficulty. Start with the first one and do not skip.

[ ]What to doWhen
[ ]Write a list of everything in the house that has a clock, a date or a chip in itby April
[ ]Find the instruction books of all of it. Put them in one drawerby April
[ ]Call or write to the makers of anything expensive. Keep the answersby May
[ ]Test the computer. First save your files on floppy disksby June
[ ]Test the video recorder and the answering machineby June
[ ]Ask the bank, in writing, how it prepares for the year 2000by July
[ ]Keep a paper copy of all bank and insurance statements from now onfrom now
[ ]Ask your doctor whether the machines at the clinic are testedby July
[ ]Check your car if it has a computer. Most older cars have noneby August
[ ]Check the heating: ask the maker of the boiler about the control panelby August
[ ]Make a first-aid kit and a box of medicines for a weekby September
[ ]Fill a box with candles, matches, batteries and a radio with batteriesby October
[ ]Buy a stove that works without electricity, and gas for itby October
[ ]Store water: ten litres per person, in closed bottlesby November
[ ]Make a list of telephone numbers on paper, not only in the machineby November
[ ]Take out a small amount of cash, enough for a weekDecember
[ ]Fill the car with petrol in the middle of December, not on the 31stDecember
[ ]Talk to the neighbours: who has a generator, who has a doctor, who has a gas stoveDecember
[ ]Switch off and unplug the computer and the video before midnightDecember 31
[ ]Celebrate the new year. Nothing may happen. That would be the best resultDecember 31

Why that order? The first six are about knowing what you have. You cannot be afraid of the right thing until you know what you own. The next ones are about money and papers, because a paper copy needs no clock. The rest is about the week after the new year: if the power goes off, the water stops and the shops close, how do you manage? The answer is, as you did your grandmother's winters: with candles, a stove and neighbours.

What I do not put in the list: I do not tell you to leave town, to buy a farm, to withdraw all your savings or to bury gold in the garden. I have read pages that say these things. I have not understood them. If the bank cannot work, a farm will not help you much either.

                                                

I am not an expert. Always check with the manufacturer. Updated 03/31/1999.
