Supplies for a week, without exaggerating

I have been through two winters with the power off for three days (the 1986 snow and the 1991 flood). That is my only qualification. Here is what I keep in a box in the cellar, and what I suggest for a family of four.

Total: about $180. The money is not wasted: everything will be used in a power cut, a flood, or a camping holiday. And the tins will be eaten sooner or later; it is the best thing about tinned food.

Water. A person needs about two litres of drinking water a day. For washing and cooking, a bit more. The cold tap will probably keep working, because the water supply of Millbrook is gravity-fed from the hill reservoir, as the town hall's engineer told me. But one cannot be sure of the pumps, so keep a stock.

Heat. Do not use a barbecue or a gas ring indoors to warm a room. Do not fall into this trap: every winter some people die of it. Use blankets and a warm room. Keep the fireplace clean if you have one.

Food that does not need cooking is a good thing to keep even if nothing happens. Bread, jam, tinned fish, dried fruit, chocolate. Chocolate is for morale.

Pets. A week's food for the cat or the dog is a good idea. And a bowl for water. Mine is a cat called Pixel; he is not worried.
