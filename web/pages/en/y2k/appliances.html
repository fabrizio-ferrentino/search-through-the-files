Appliances: what I tested in my own house

I went through the house with a pencil and wrote down everything with a clock. It was thirty-one things. Here are the ones I have tested or written to the makers about. The brands are the ones in my house; yours will be different. A verdict of OK only means that it worked for me, on the day I tested it. I am not a laboratory.

FAILED = DO NOT TRUST THE DATE ON THAT MACHINE AFTER MIDNIGHT

What does "compliant" mean? It means that the maker says the product will work correctly with dates after December 31, 1999. It does not mean that the maker has tested it; it does not mean that the machine works with other machines. I asked a maker for a written statement and received a card with a smiling sun on it and the words "Our products are ready for the future." I keep it as a souvenir.

The tests. Method: unplug everything else, save your work, set the date to the last day of the year about two minutes before midnight, wait, look. Then set the date back and, if the machine allows, restart it. Do not do it on a machine that holds something precious without a copy. Do not test the heating controller unless you can reset it by hand. I did, and I spent a cold evening finding the instruction booklet.

Things I did not test: the lift in the town hall (the janitor says it is safe), the traffic lights at the bridge (the council says it is safe), my pacemaker (I do not have one, but my uncle does, and he says the cardiologist laughed).

                                                

I am not an expert. Always check with the manufacturer. Updated 03/31/1999.
