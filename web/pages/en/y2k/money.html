Money and papers

My bank is a small one, on the square. The manager is a nice man who told me with a smile that "everything has been taken care of". I asked if he could write that down. He said he would think about it. That is where my worries about money began. Here is what I did, and what I suggest.

1. Ask your bank, in writing
  • Is your computer system ready for the year 2000? Have you tested it?
  • On what date was the test?
  • Who is responsible if there is an error in my account?
  • What will happen to my cash card on January 1?

Keep the reply with the bank statements. If you do not receive a reply within a month, ask again, and if you still do not, think about it.

2. Keep a paper record

From now on, ask for a printed statement every month. Keep them in a box. In January 2000, compare the last statement of 1999 with the first of 2000. If they agree, you can relax.

3. Cash

A small amount of cash at home, enough for a week of shopping, is wise at any time of the year. I do not tell you to withdraw everything. That would be silly and would also make the bank worry, which is the opposite of what we want.

4. Pension, taxes, insurance

The pension office has said that its computers are ready. I have written to ask in which way. For the taxes, keep a copy of every form. For the insurance, make sure that the policy has a date of expiry that is not the thirty-first of December: some old policies have a date of expiry in two digits, and a computer may think the policy ended in 1900.

5. Your computer files

If you have a home computer with a spreadsheet or a small accounting program, make a copy on a floppy disk of all your work before the end of the year. Then print the important pages. A paper copy needs neither a clock nor a computer.

6. The famous cash machines

The cash machine outside my bank has a sticker that says "Year 2000 ready". I have not tried it yet. My plan is to withdraw a small sum on January 2, and one on January 3, and see if the number of the receipt makes sense.

                                                

I am not an expert. Always check with the manufacturer. Updated 03/31/1999.
