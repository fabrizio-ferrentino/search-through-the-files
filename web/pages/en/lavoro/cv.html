How to write your CV

  1. One page. Two at most. Nobody reads the third.
  2. Personal details at the top: name, address, phone. A photo only if requested.
  3. Experience, from the most recent. For each job: when, where, what you did. Use verbs: "organised", "managed", "solved".
  4. Education: the highest qualification, and courses that matter for the job.
  5. Skills: languages, computers, driving licence.
  6. Interests: two or three, true ones. "Reading and travelling" says nothing. "Amateur football coach" says a lot.

Gaps in your CV: if you have been out of work, or have done the same job for years, say so honestly and say what you learned. Recruiters see a lot of CVs; they recognise the ones that pretend.

Night work: many people who have worked nights for years find it hard to move to day jobs. Our advice: underline what night work teaches - responsibility, autonomy, reliability. It is experience like any other.
