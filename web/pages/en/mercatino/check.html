How to check a used computer

Switch it on in front of you. Listen: the fan should hum, the disk should whirr and then calm down. A disk that clicks, scratches or goes silent is a disk that is about to die.

Look at the date. Ask the seller to open the system clock. If the date is wrong by years, the battery inside is flat: cheap to replace, but it tells you the computer has spent a long time switched off. Or switched on, alone, somewhere.

Open a few programs. The word processor, the calculator, a game. Count how long they take. Then ask to see the disk: how full it is, what is on it. A computer full of somebody else's files is a computer that was never cleaned.

Ask why it is being sold. "We bought a new one" is the best answer. "Personal reasons" is a fine answer too, most of the time. Look at the seller while they answer.

Format the disk. Always, before you start using it. Your files deserve a clean house. And you do not want to find, one night, a letter that somebody else wrote.

Last tip. A good computer can be ten years old. A bad one can be ten months old. The age matters less than the way it was used, and by whom.
