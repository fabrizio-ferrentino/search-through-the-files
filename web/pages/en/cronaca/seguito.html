Suburb tragedy: no charges for the neighbour
Prosecutor: "He acted to save the boy's life". The son is out of danger and will be placed with relatives. The question of why remains unanswered.

There will be no proceedings against the retired man who, on Tuesday night, stopped the father who had just killed his wife and was attacking his elder son in their suburban home. The prosecutor's office announced it yesterday at the end of its inquiries: "The neighbour stepped in to save a boy's life. The attacker's death was the result of his fall down the stairs during the struggle".

The old man, who has not left his house for days, gave his lawyer a few words to pass on: "I did what anybody would have done. But it wasn't him. I had known him for years, and the man on that landing wasn't him. He had a face I had never seen on him. I won't forget it as long as I live".

Good news, meanwhile, from the county hospital: the boy has been declared out of danger and will leave the ward in the next few days. The family court has ordered that the couple's children be placed with an aunt and an uncle, who live in another county and have already come to the hospital.

The computer. Among the items taken from the house was the man's personal computer, which according to the neighbours stayed on until dawn. Investigators examined it without finding, as far as we know, anything useful to the inquiry: "Letters, notes, household accounts, a diary. Nothing that explains something like this". The computer, along with the other personal belongings, was returned to the family once the case was closed.

In the neighbourhood, one question remains that nobody can answer. In front of the house, still sealed, someone has left flowers and a handwritten note: "Why?". The parish priest will hold the woman's funeral on Saturday. For the man's, the family has asked for privacy.

To protect the children, the Herald is not publishing the names of the people involved.
