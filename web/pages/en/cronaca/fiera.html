Autumn fair breaks attendance record, rains only once
Stalls, rides, the cake contest and the brass band: more than twenty thousand people in three days. Organisers: "We have never seen so many people".

The thirtieth Autumn Fair closed last night with fireworks over the sports ground. It was a record-breaking edition: according to the organisers, more than twenty thousand people passed through the old town over the three days, almost twice as many as last year. Part of the credit goes to the weather, which allowed only one downpour on Saturday afternoon, when stallholders and visitors took shelter under the arcades of the square.

There were a hundred and twenty stalls, from cheeses of the valleys to woodcraft, local sweets and, new this year, a stand of second-hand computers and video games that delighted the youngsters. There were queues at the funfair rides, set up as always in the station car park, and at the parish raffle, which raised over fifteen hundred dollars for the children's summer camp.

The cake contest. The most awaited moment of Sunday, the traditional home-made cake contest, had forty-two entries. For the third year running the winner was an eighty-one-year-old lady with her plum tart: "The secret? Good butter and patience. And don't tell anyone". The jury, made up of the mayor, the bandmaster and two children picked by lot, gave second place to an apple pie and the special prize to a cake shaped like a tractor.

The organising committee is delighted and thanks the civil protection volunteers and the traffic wardens. "Next year we would like to extend the fair into the park", says the chairman. "People want to be together. It is lovely to see the families in the square, the grandparents with the grandchildren. That is why we do it".
