Let me explain the name, since it is the only thing on this page I did not make myself.

Behind my building there is the railway. There used to be a little station too, two platforms and a bench, but in 1993 they closed it: not enough passengers. The platforms are still there. The clock is still there, stopped at ten past six, and nobody knows if it was morning or evening when it stopped. The trains go by without slowing down.

The last one is the 23:52. A regional train, four carriages, all lit up. When I come home from the shift I sometimes go and sit on the bench and wait for it. It goes past in four seconds. In four seconds you see maybe thirty windows and thirty people inside, reading, sleeping, leaning on somebody. I try to count the faces. Nobody ever looks out. Why would they. There is nothing here. There is a bench and a man.

when i was a boy my uncle used to say: the train only comes by once in life, you have to jump on.

nobody told me it does not stop at every station.
nobody told me what you do if it does not stop at yours.

At work there is an older man, the one who drives the forklift, who said it to me last month without even looking up: "at your age, son, the train has gone". He was talking about women. He laughed. He meant it kindly, I think. I went home and found the old 1993 timetable in a drawer, the last one with our station still printed in it, and I pinned it above the monitor. It is still there. It has gone yellow.

So that is my name. I am not angry with the train. I think it does not even know we are here. I think it just goes where it was told to go, and the people inside are going home, and home is somewhere else.

But I keep going to the bench. Every night it passes, I am there. This page is a bit like that bench: if you are reading it, in a way, the train slowed down.

If anyone knows whether they will ever open the station again, please write it in the guestbook. I asked at the ticket office in town. They looked at me as if I had asked about the moon.
