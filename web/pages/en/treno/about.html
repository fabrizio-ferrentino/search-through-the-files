I am not putting my name here. It is not a secret, it is just that nobody uses it anyway. At work they call me "the night guy". At the bar downstairs I am "the usual". Here I am missedtrain, and that is fine.

I am at the age when everybody else gets married. I know because in the last two years six invitations arrived, and on each one there was my name and nothing else, no "and guest". I went to two. They seat the single ones next to the little cousins, and at least the little cousins talk.

workwarehouse, night shift. Loading, unloading, checking the delivery notes, switching off the lights when the others leave and switching them on again when they come back. I like the night because at night nobody notices you are alone: everybody is.
homea studio flat on the third floor. From the window I can see the building across the street: twelve windows, twelve families. In the evening they all light up together, around eight, when people eat.
i likethe sea in winter, old radios, nature documentaries, the sound of the modem connecting (it sounds like somebody answering), night trains. One train in particular.
i don't likeSundays. At lunch everybody has somebody. Even the TV adverts on Sundays are all about families.
looking forI don't know. Someone to talk to. Someone who remembers my name.

When I was a boy they told me not to complain. My father used to say that men don't cry and don't ask, they manage. So I managed. I learned to cook for one, to talk little, to smile at weddings. I learned that if you never ask for anything, nobody ever says no.

Then the Internet arrived and everybody said: now nobody will ever be alone again, now you can talk to the whole world. I believed it. I bought the modem in instalments. The whole world is there, it is true. It is just that the whole world is busy.

But I don't want this page to be sad. This page is my place. If you want to write to me, the guestbook is open, always.
