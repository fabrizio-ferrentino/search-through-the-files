Welcome to the library

Twenty-eight thousand books, two hundred videotapes, the local newspapers since 1952 and, since last autumn, two computers connected to the Internet. Membership is free for everyone who lives or works in Millbrook and the villages around it.

The library is a quiet place, but not a silent one. You can talk at a low voice, you can study in groups in the back room, and you can ask the librarians anything: if they do not know the answer, they know which book has it.

INTERNET
CORNER		 Our two computers can be booked at the desk for one hour at a time. Children under fourteen must be accompanied by an adult. Please do not change the settings: last week someone set the screen to green letters on black, and a pensioner thought the computer had broken.

Thursday evening the reading room stays open until ten for students and for anyone who needs a quiet, warm place to read. The loan desk closes at seven as usual.

The book drop. When the library is closed you can return books through the slot in the door. Do not use it for videotapes: they break. Do not use it for letters to the mayor either: that is the next door.

How to borrow

BORROWER'S
CARD		 Books: three, for thirty days.
Videotapes: two, for seven days.
Renewals: one, if nobody has reserved it.
Fines: none.

Membership. Bring an identity document to the desk: the card is made on the spot and is free. Children under fourteen need the signature of a parent.

How many and for how long. Up to three books for thirty days, and two videotapes for seven days. You can renew a loan once, by phone or at the desk, if nobody else has reserved the book.

Late returns. We do not charge fines. We send a postcard. After the third postcard, the librarian calls you. After the librarian, nobody has ever needed anything else.

Our history

The library was born in 1921 in two rooms above the old school, with the four hundred books donated by the town doctor when he retired. His bookplate - a small owl and the words read in the evening - is still inside many of the oldest volumes.

Today the library has about two thousand members. The most borrowed section is the novels; the second, to everyone's surprise, is cooking. The most stolen book in our history is a guide to the region's mushrooms, which we have bought seven times.
