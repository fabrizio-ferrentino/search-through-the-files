Our history

The library was born in 1921 in two rooms above the old school, with the four hundred books donated by the town doctor when he retired. His bookplate - a small owl and the words read in the evening - is still inside many of the oldest volumes.

In 1966 the flood reached the ground floor of the school and destroyed a third of the books. The photographs of that autumn show the volunteers laying the wet books open on the tables of the square, in the sun, one page at a time. Many were saved; the ones that could not be saved were replaced with books donated from all over the country.

In 1983 the library moved here, into the former grain store next to the town hall: thick walls, high ceilings, and in winter a cold that no radiator has ever fully defeated. In 1998 the two computers arrived, a gift from a local bank, and with them this site, built by the participants of our first computer course.

Today the library has about two thousand members. The most borrowed section is the novels; the second, to everyone's surprise, is cooking. The most stolen book in our history is a guide to the region's mushrooms, which we have bought seven times.

If you have photographs or memories of the library in the past, we would be glad to have them for the local history afternoon in May.
