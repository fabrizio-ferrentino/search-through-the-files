The town

Millbrook grew around a water mill on the river, which is still there, without its wheel, behind the bakery. In the last century the brick factory brought work and families; when it closed in 1975, many left, and the town shrank. Since the new road to the city opened, young families have started to come back: the new housing estate on the edge of town, built in 1992, is full of children, bicycles and barbecues.

To see: the church of the patron saint with its leaning bell tower; the lake, with the path all around it (two hours on foot, three with children); the old mill; and, on clear days, the mountains from the bridge.

To eat: lake fish at the restaurant by the jetty, pizza at the Station Bar on Saturday, and the cakes of the spring market, if you get there before ten.

The lake is beautiful and cold. Swimming is allowed only at the beach with the lifeguard, from June to September. Every year someone tries elsewhere, and every year the council repeats it.
