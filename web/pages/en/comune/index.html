Official pages of the town council

Welcome to the Town of Millbrook

Millbrook, 6,400 inhabitants, a lake, a river, three schools, a library, a football ground and a town hall that has been "temporarily" in the old school building since 1971. This site is the town hall's window on the Internet: here you find opening hours, notices, events and the news of the council.

  • Technical office (building permits, roads): Tue and Thu 9 AM - 12 PM
  • Social services: Mon, Wed, Fri 9 AM - 12 PM, by appointment
  • Tax office: Mon-Fri 9 AM - 12 PM
  • Agriculture office: Wed 9 AM - 12 PM
  • Municipal police: every day 8 AM - 8 PM
  • The mayor: Saturday morning, by appointment

Social services also help people who live alone and need company or a hand with shopping: the volunteers of the parish and of the pensioners' association visit them once a week. If you know someone who needs it, tell us. If it is you, tell us anyway: it is not a shame.

Offices are closed on public holidays and on the feast of the patron saint.

Certificates and documents

Online certificate requests are not active. The regional project to issue certificates through the Internet has been postponed. For now, certificates are requested at the registry office, in person.

Residence and family certificates are issued on the spot. Bring an identity document. If you request a certificate for someone else, you need their signed authorisation.

The identity card is valid for five years. Bring three passport photographs, the old card and, if you have lost it, the police report.

Waste collection

The weekly timetable of the collection:

  • Monday: paper and cardboard
  • Tuesday: general waste
  • Wednesday: glass and cans
  • Thursday: plastic
  • Friday: general waste
  • Saturday: garden waste (from April to October)

Put the bags out after 8 PM the evening before, not before: the cats of the town are organised, and they know the timetable better than you.

Large items (furniture, old appliances, computers): call the technical office to book a free collection. Do not leave them next to the bins. Last month someone left a whole computer, screen and keyboard, next to the bins in Lake Road. It was gone the next morning before the lorry arrived. We do not know who took it.

Batteries and medicines go in the special containers at the chemist's and at the school.

The town

Millbrook grew around a water mill on the river, which is still there, without its wheel, behind the bakery. In the last century the brick factory brought work and families; when it closed in 1975, many left, and the town shrank. Since the new road to the city opened, young families have started to come back: the new housing estate on the edge of town, built in 1992, is full of children, bicycles and barbecues.

To see: the church of the patron saint with its leaning bell tower; the lake, with the path all around it (two hours on foot, three with children); the old mill; and, on clear days, the mountains from the bridge.

To eat: lake fish at the restaurant by the jetty, pizza at the Station Bar on Saturday, and the cakes of the spring market, if you get there before ten.

The lake is beautiful and cold. Swimming is allowed only at the beach with the lifeguard, from June to September. Every year someone tries elsewhere, and every year the council repeats it.
