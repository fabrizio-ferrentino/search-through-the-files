How to read the table. Min is the lowest temperature of the night before, Max the highest of the day. The wind is given with its direction (where it comes from) and its average speed; gusts can be twice as strong.

SEA AND MOUNTAINS

Avalanche risk is measured on a scale from 1 to 5. At 3 the snow is unstable on many slopes: stay on the marked runs and do not go off-piste alone. Spring snow is the most treacherous: firm in the morning, heavy and wet in the afternoon, when most accidents happen.

For sailors. The sea state is given on the Douglas scale: 3 is slight, 4 moderate, 5 rough. A sea that looks calm from the beach can be very different a mile out: listen to the marine forecast on the radio before you go out, and tell someone ashore when you expect to be back.

THE SEASON AHEAD: APRIL, MAY, JUNE 1999

Seasonal forecasts are not forecasts in the usual sense: nobody can tell you whether it will rain on the fifteenth of May. They say whether the season will be, on average, warmer or colder, wetter or drier than usual. Take them for what they are: a tendency.

Month Temperature Rain Our note
April a little below average above average a typical April: four seasons in a day
May average average the best month for a trip
June above average below average the first heat arrives early

Last year. Our seasonal forecast for the summer of 1998 said "hot and dry". It was hot and dry. Our forecast for the autumn said "mild". It snowed in November. We report both, because a site that only tells you when it was right is not a site to trust.

The gardener's calendar. Plant tomatoes outside only after the middle of April, when the nights stay above ten degrees. Courgettes and beans can wait for May. The old saying is right more often than we are: wait until the late frosts are over before you put away the winter coats - and the plants' covers.

THE SATELLITE IMAGE

A tip from our forecaster: look at the direction the clouds are moving in the animation, not just where they are. A large white mass far away to the west, moving east, is your afternoon. A small one right above you, moving away, is already over.

THE WORDS OF THE WEATHER

Anticyclone - an area of high pressure. It brings stable, dry weather: sun in summer, fog and cold in winter.

Depression - an area of low pressure, the opposite. Clouds, wind, rain. Also what readers feel after a week of it.

Cold front - the edge of a mass of cold air that pushes under warm air. Short, violent showers, then clear skies and a drop in temperature.

Warm front - the edge of warm air sliding over cold air. Long, steady rain, grey skies, a slow rise in temperature.

Isobars - the lines on the map that join points with the same pressure. The closer they are, the stronger the wind.

Dew point - the temperature at which the air can no longer hold its water. When the night falls below it, there is dew on the grass, or fog.

Foehn - a dry, warm wind that comes down the mountains. It melts the snow in a day and, the old people say, gives everyone a headache.

Precipitation - anything that falls from the sky: rain, snow, sleet, hail. Not frogs. We have been asked.

Probability of rain - "40%" does not mean it will rain for 40% of the day. It means that on ten days like this one, it rains on four.
