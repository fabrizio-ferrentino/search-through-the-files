We are sorry. The dish that received the satellite pictures was on the roof of our old office, and the new roof is not ready yet. In the meantime, here is how to read the image when it comes back.

White means high, thick clouds: storms, heavy rain, snow in winter. Grey means low clouds and fog, which bring drizzle but rarely real rain. Black is clear sky, over the sea or over the land. The image is taken every half hour; the animation shows the last twelve, that is the last six hours.

A tip from our forecaster: look at the direction the clouds are moving in the animation, not just where they are. A large white mass far away to the west, moving east, is your afternoon. A small one right above you, moving away, is already over.

Many readers ask why the image is in black and white. The satellite does not see colours: it measures how much heat each point gives off. Cold means high, high means clouds. The colour pictures you see on television are painted afterwards, by a computer, to make them prettier.
