How it worked

The telephone. From 10 PM to 6 AM, every night, two volunteers answered in turn. You did not have to give your name. You could call to talk about something terrible, or about nothing at all: the weather, a film, the silence in the house. Both are fine. Both are why we were there.

The message board. You wrote a message, signed with any name you liked. A volunteer read it and answered on the board, within three or four days. The messages are numbered so that you could find yours again.

What we did not do. We did not judge, we did not preach, we did not tell anyone what to do with their life. We did not pretend to be doctors. When someone was in danger, we said so clearly, and we gave them the numbers of people who could do more than us.

What we learned. Most of the people who called us were not desperate. They were alone. They had a job, a flat, a family somewhere. And nobody, in the whole day, had asked them how they were. Many men, in particular, told us that we were the first people they had ever said certain things to.
