an essay on facial proportion and its consequences

Introduction

Every age has believed that the soul can be read in the face. The ancients read it in the shape of the skull, the last century in the bumps of the head. They were wrong in the details and right in the principle. This essay corrects the details.

The thesis is simple. A small number of measurements, taken with a ruler and a mirror, describe a face better than any adjective. Those same measurements predict, with a precision that should embarrass the romantic, how that face will be received by others: in work, in friendship and above all in love. The face is not a mirror of the soul. The face is a sentence that has already been passed.


Fig. 1 - The five fundamental measurements. 1 interocular distance, 2 middle third, 3 bizygomatic width, 4 total height, 5 mandibular angle.

I will be accused of cruelty. I answer that cruelty belongs to the world, not to whoever measures it. A thermometer is not responsible for the fever. Those who write to me (and many do, as you will see in the correspondence) thank me for the same reason: it is better to know the temperature than to be told that you are just a little tired.

Read the method first, then measure, then consult the tables. Do not skip steps. Those who skip steps always find a better number than the true one.
