1. The method

You will need: a transparent ruler with millimetres, a mirror at least as wide as your shoulders, a desk lamp, a pencil, a sheet of paper and about an hour of honesty. Do not ask anyone to help you: the presence of another person makes us hold our faces differently.

1.1 Position. Sit facing the mirror, the lamp to your left, the head straight. Relax the jaw. Do not smile: a smile is a mask that the face puts on for others, and here we are measuring the face others see when the mask slips.

1.2 The five measurements. Take each measurement three times and write down the average. Measurement 1 is the distance between the centres of the pupils. Measurement 2 goes from the root of the nose to the base of the nose. Measurement 3 is the width of the face at the cheekbones. Measurement 4 is the total height, from the hairline to the chin. Measurement 5 is the angle of the jaw, estimated with the corner of the sheet of paper.

1.3 The ratios. Divide 3 by 4, then 1 by 3, then 2 by 4. Write the three numbers with two decimals. These are your ratios. Your name is no longer necessary.

1.4 The score. Compare each ratio with the ideal values in the tables and add up the deviations as explained there. The result is a number from 0 to 10. Write it on the sheet, fold it, keep it. Many readers have told me that they kept the sheet in their wallet for months. I do not recommend it, but I understand it.

1.5 What the method does not measure. Kindness, intelligence, a good voice, the ability to listen. I have been asked many times why I leave them out. The answer is in the tables: nobody else measures them either. Not when it matters.
