3. Readers' faces

With their permission, I publish some of the measurements that readers have sent me, without names. They are the most useful page of the essay: here the tables stop being numbers and become people. Or the other way round.

Reader 7 · accountant, 31 · score: 5.2 (invisible)

He sent a second letter to ask if a beard would help. A beard hides measurement 5. It does not change it.

Reader 13 · student, 22 · score: 6.4 (acceptable)

He wrote that he was relieved. I did not have the heart to tell him that 6.4 at twenty-two is 5.8 at thirty.

Reader 19 · bus driver, 40 · score: 3.6 (excluded)

He measured himself and his son. The son scored 3.7. He asked me if it is hereditary. It is.

Reader 31 · unemployed, 27 · score: 4.1 (invisible)

He asked me to remove his measurements from the site. I removed his name. The measurements belong to science.

Reader 44 · clerk, 35 · score: 2.9 (excluded)

The lowest score I have ever recorded. He thanked me. He said that at last nobody could tell him he had not tried hard enough.

Reader 52 · - · score: not measurable (-)

The envelope arrived on 11/21/1998, without a sender. The postmark was from June. Inside there was a photograph of a man sitting on a bench at a railway station at night. Where the face should be, the photograph is simply white. Not damaged: white. I have measured it anyway, out of habit. There is nothing to measure. I keep it in the drawer and I do not like the drawer any more.
