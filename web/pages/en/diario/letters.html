letters I did not send

Three letters. Written, copied out properly, put in envelopes, never posted. I publish them because a letter that nobody reads is not finished.

To C., sixteen years old, the desk in front of mine

You lent me a pen in the maths exam in 1974 and you never asked for it back. I still have it. I wanted to tell you that it was the first time somebody gave me something without being asked. I did not tell you. You moved to another city that summer. I think you would not remember the pen. That is all right. I remember it for both of us.

To R., the office where I worked before teaching

We had lunch together every day for two years, at the same table, by the window. I thought it meant something and I was afraid that if I asked, it would stop meaning it. Then you got married, and you invited me, and at the wedding you introduced me to your husband as 'my lunch'. I have never been so happy and so sad in the same second.

To the neighbour on the second floor

You knocked once, the night of the blackout, to ask if I had candles. I had candles. I gave you four. You said: you are always so prepared. I wanted to say: I have had a lot of time to prepare. Instead I said goodnight. You moved out in the spring. The new neighbour has never knocked.
