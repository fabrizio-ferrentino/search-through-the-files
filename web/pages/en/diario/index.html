days since my eighteenth birthday

That is the number. I update it by hand, on Sundays, adding seven. It counts the days since I turned eighteen, which is the age at which they tell you that life starts. It does not count anything else, because there is nothing else to count. No first date, no first kiss, no hand held in a cinema. Days without.

I am forty. I am not writing this to be pitied. I teach geography at a technical institute, I have a good bicycle, I go to my sister's on Sundays and I am the uncle who brings the cake. I am, as everybody says, a good man. Good men, I have learned, are the ones who are invited to everything and taken home by nobody.

I started this page because a colleague of mine, a young one, said that on the Internet everybody has a diary now. I thought: then nobody will notice one more. That is the idea. A diary that nobody notices, written by a man nobody noticed. It seemed honest.

You will find the entries of 1998, those of this year, a list I keep, and three letters that I wrote and never posted. The last update is the most recent page. Read it last.
