From the archive: two threads worth keeping

Pentium II or K6-2 for a new build? (pixel77, 03/31/1999 - 04/02/1999, 9 replies, archived). pixel77 wanted to retire his old 486 and build a new PC for games, his little brother's homework and the Internet. The shop offered a Pentium II 350 with 64 MB and a 6 GB disk, or a K6-2 350 with 64 MB, an 8 GB disk and a 3D card for the same price. the_defragmenter: "For games, the 3D card makes a bigger difference than the processor. Kit B, no doubts." kernel_sam: "The Pentium II is faster with floating point, and some new games really want it. But at the same price, with a 3D card thrown in... yes, B. I hate admitting it." zaphod99: "Wait for the year 2000, everything will cost half. Or the world will end. Either way you save money." The moderator's advice for any first build: buy a decent power supply, because the cheap ones die and take something with them, and buy a case you can open without a screwdriver and without bleeding. You will open it a hundred times. Final post by pixel77: "Thanks everyone, this was the most peaceful processor thread in the history of the internet."

56k modem only connects at 28.8 (Lady_Baud, 03/29/1999, 7 replies, archived, solved). A brand new external 56k modem that connects at 28,800 at best, some nights 26,400, worse than the old 33.6. doc_modem: "56k only works if the line between you and the telephone exchange is clean, and most home lines are not." His list, in order: download the newest drivers from the manufacturer's site, not the ones on the CD; plug the modem directly into the wall socket, with no extension cables or splitters; unplug anything else on the same line (fax, answering machine, a second phone, an alarm system); and pick up a phone while nobody is calling and listen for a hiss or a hum, in which case call the phone company, not the provider. kernel_sam, who lives at the end of a country road: "I have never seen anything above 31,200 in my life. I made peace with it. I read a lot of books while pages load." The culprit, in the end, was the fax machine.
